**Finding, Q2 2026:** Digital Realty's new leases signed in the Americas were priced at
$293 per kW for deals of 0–1 MW, and $156 per kW for deals over 1 MW.

## What the filing shows

| New Americas leases signed, Q2 2026 | Capacity signed | Annualized GAAP base rent | Per kW |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–1 MW | 10.6 MW | $37.1 million | $293 |
| Over 1 MW | 44.2 MW | $82.7 million | $156 |

The filing's table heads the last column "GAAP Base Rent per Kilowatt" without saying per what
period. Dividing annualized rent by kilowatts and by 12 reproduces both figures ($291.9 and
$155.9), so they are per kW **per month**. That is DCU's check, not the filing's wording.

## What this is, and what it isn't

- **One landlord, one quarter, the Americas only.** It is not a market average and is not
  comparable with another landlord's figures.
- **Signed leases, not asking rent.** These are deals Digital Realty closed, not rates it quotes.
- **GAAP rent.** GAAP spreads the rent over the whole lease term, escalators included, so it
  differs from the cash rent paid in the first year.
- **An average across different deals.** Location, power density and contract terms all move it.

## If you are pricing a requirement under 1 MW

One landlord's signed deals put small requirements at nearly twice the rent per kW of large
ones. When you compare quotes, check:

1. Whether each is stated per kW per month, and whether it is cash rent or GAAP (straight-line) rent.
2. The escalator and the term, since both change the average.
3. What each quote's rent includes and what is charged separately.
