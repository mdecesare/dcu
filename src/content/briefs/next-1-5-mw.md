For an enterprise-owned data center, another 1–5 MW can decide when a business initiative moves
forward: a bank expanding digital services, a university adding research computing, or a
pharmaceutical company putting AI to work.

These facilities support essential workloads. When the utility's schedule falls behind the
business schedule, owners need a credible way forward that protects uptime, meets local
requirements, and fits the facility's long-term plans.

<div><h3>Battery storage</h3><p>Energy for selected hours</p><span>Size the duration, and the charging supply.</span></div>
</div>

</figure>

On-site generation and storage deserve an early look. The starting point is the additional
facility load, including cooling, and whether that demand runs continuously or only during
certain hours.

**Generator systems can provide sustained power when selected for the intended duty.**
Manufacturers rate the same generator differently for standby, prime and continuous duty:
Caterpillar, for example, defines standby around limited annual hours, and prime and continuous
around unlimited hours at varying and steady loads.<sup><a href="#source-1" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>
Gas-fired systems also depend on adequate gas service. Existing emergency generators should not
be assumed suitable or authorized for everyday operation; their ratings and operating
restrictions need checking.<sup><a href="#source-2" aria-label="Source 2">2</a></sup>

**Fuel cells offer another route to continuous generation.** Commercial offerings include
FuelCell Energy's 1.25 MW and 2.5 MW (nameplate) systems and Bloom's modular Energy Server,
which Bloom describes as operating 24/7.<sup><a href="#source-3" aria-label="Source 3">3</a>, <a href="#source-4" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup>
Evaluate the proposed footprint, emissions, fuel supply, service support and lifecycle cost
against your site's needs. A published product specification is a starting point for that
conversation, not a commitment.

**Battery storage can strengthen the overall system.** Depending on the design, it can help
cover demand peaks and support transitions between power sources. But it needs energy to
recharge. For an added load that runs continuously, storage must be paired with sufficient
utility supply or generation.<sup><a href="#source-5" aria-label="Source 5">5</a>, <a href="#source-6" aria-label="Source 6">6</a></sup>

The equipment is only part of the project. Before committing, establish:<sup><a href="#source-7" aria-label="Source 7">7</a></sup>

- The fuel supply and approvals needed for the proposed operating hours.
- How generation, UPS, batteries, switchgear, controls and cooling will work together through
  maintenance and failures.
- The complete installation schedule, operating costs, and plan for connecting to future
  utility service.

For the owner, the goal is to protect the business schedule while preserving the facility's
reliability objectives. A system designed for both independent and grid-connected operation may
also have a useful role after the utility upgrade, subject to its design and operating
permissions.<sup><a href="#source-6" aria-label="Source 6">6</a></sup>

<div class="bf-closing">

A delayed utility upgrade can be a serious setback, but it doesn't automatically mean putting
the expansion on hold. Depending on your site, fuel supply and local requirements, on-site power
may provide a practical bridge while utility capacity catches up.

Exploring those options early gives your team more room to make a sound decision, and may give
the business a way to move forward sooner.
