**Finding, 17 September 2026:** an emergency order from the U.S. Department of Energy authorized
PJM, the grid operator for Virginia, Ohio and other states, to direct backup generation to run
as a last resort. The order was effective 17 through 18 September 2026 and has expired.

## What the order allowed

- PJM was authorized to direct backup generation resources ("whether auxiliary, standby,
  directly-connected, battery storage, or other") to operate **as a last resort** before declaring,
  or during, an EEA 3: the emergency level at which firm load can be interrupted.
- Backup generation serving a critical reliability or backup need was **excluded**, including
  hospitals, 911 call centers, water and wastewater treatment, and first responders.
- Any operation was limited to the times and conditions PJM set for reliability, and PJM had to
  report daily to DOE which backup resources operated under the order.
- PJM's request and the order's background refer to backup generation **at large loads**. The
  operative paragraph itself says backup generation resources, without a size.

## What this is, and what it isn't

- **An authorization.** It is not evidence that any particular site ran its generators. DCU has
  not seen PJM's reports to DOE and has no record of which sites, if any, were called.
- **Dated and expired.** The order required any renewal to be requested before it expired.

## If your facility is on PJM's grid and has standby generation, check

1. Fuel on hand, and how quickly you can be resupplied.
2. Your permitted run-hours, and what an emergency run counts against.
3. How your utility would reach you, and who on your side would answer.
4. Whether your backup serves a need the order excluded.
