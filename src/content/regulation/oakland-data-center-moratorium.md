**Status:** In effect · extension passed 10 November 2026; runs from 20 November 2026 for ten months and fifteen days, to about 5 October 2027 · **Next:** permanent data center regulations · **Checked:** 13 November 2026

> **If your organization has, or plans, a server room in Oakland.** The moratorium exempts server rooms
> that serve the building's own business, but only if they rely *solely* on the building's existing
> water, HVAC and electrical supply. A project that needs new electrical service, new cooling or new
> backup generation could fall under the moratorium. Ask Oakland Planning before you design or file.

## What it does

While the moratorium lasts, the city "shall not issue or approve any building permit, occupancy permit,
conditional use permit, variance, subdivision map or other land use entitlement ... or permit or
regulatory license" for "the construction, operation or development of a data center" by any private
or public entity anywhere in the city.

The original 45-day moratorium (Ordinance No. 13899 C.M.S.) expires on 20 November 2026. This ordinance
extends it for ten months and fifteen days from that date, unless the Council adopts permanent
regulations sooner. State law (Government Code section 65858) allows one further extension.

## Who it affects

A "data center" is a building, facility or structure used to store, manage, process and transmit
digital data, with its computer and network equipment and "associated power and accessory
infrastructure such as cooling systems, power generators, backup generators, or electrical
substations." The ordinance sets no size threshold.

**Exempt:** "server rooms, accessory server rooms, data processing equipment, or information technology
facilities that are incidental, subordinate, or specifically related to a permitted principal use and
relies solely on the principal use's existing water, heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC),
and electrical supply." Running a server room for purposes unrelated to the property's principal use is
prohibited.

## If you have a project in Oakland, check

1. Whether your server room or IT space would need any new electrical service, cooling or generation.
   If it would, it may not qualify for the exemption.
2. How the City Administrator, who may issue interpretations under the ordinance, reads "existing"
   supply for an upgrade or replacement.
3. What permanent regulations the city proposes before the moratorium ends.
