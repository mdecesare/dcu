**Status:** In effect · passed by the Common Council 13 October 2026, signed by the mayor 14 October, published 16 October · runs to 13 October 2027 at the latest · **Next:** zoning framework (file 260142), still in committee · **Checked:** 13 November 2026

> **For an existing enterprise facility.** The pause covers *enlarging* an existing data center,
> not only building new ones. A server room inside an office, hospital or other building with a
> different principal use is excluded. A standalone enterprise data center planning an addition in
> Milwaukee cannot get city approval for it while the pause lasts.

## What it does

The ordinance prohibits "the establishment of new data centers or enlargement of existing data
centers" while it is in effect. No city department, board or officer may approve an application,
permit, occupancy, zoning or site-plan request "to establish, construct, enlarge, expand, convert, or
occupy a data center." The prohibition applies however the facility is described in an application,
if its principal use meets the definition. It does not apply to planned development districts.

It lasts for the lesser of one year after passage, or until the zoning code is amended to regulate
data centers. A separate file (260142) proposes that zoning framework; it was still in committee when we
checked.

## Who it affects

A "data center" is a facility used primarily to operate, maintain or provide access to computer and
network facilities, together with accessory uses such as offices, air handlers, power generators,
generator fuel storage, water cooling and utility infrastructure. It **does not** mean "a computer
server room or a facility that serves another principal use on the same premises." The ordinance sets
no size threshold.

## If you operate or plan a data center in Milwaukee, check

1. Whether your facility's principal use is the data center, or whether it is accessory to another use.
2. Whether any permit or approval you need for an addition, conversion or new equipment yard is now on
   hold, and whether your site is in a planned development district.
3. Whether file 260142, the zoning framework, sets standards that would apply after the pause.
