**Status:** In effect · **Applies to:** applications submitted on or after 1 July 2026

## What changed

Virginia wrote a data center-specific emissions requirement into statute. It is
not agency guidance that DEQ could revise on its own — it is § 10.1-1322.6,
enacted as HB 507 in the 2026 session.

## Who it applies to

Data center air permit applications submitted to DEQ on or after 1 July 2026.

Two things worth knowing about the edges.

Applications submitted before that date are not covered. And the statute does
not itself create a permit requirement — it governs permits that are already
required, where the application is submitted on or after 1 July 2026. A project
that needs no air permit is outside it.

On exemptions: Virginia's [minor NSR permit exemptions](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title9/agency5/chapter80/section1105/) list,
among other categories, diesel engines with an aggregate rated brake horsepower
of less than 1,675, and diesel engines powering electrical generators with an
aggregate rated electrical output of less than 1,125 kW — in each case subject to
the regulation's other conditions. A project above those size thresholds is not
automatically outside the exemptions: the regulation also provides a separate
route that turns on uncontrolled emission rates rather than size, with its own
conditions. Neither route is automatic, and which applies depends on the
application.

## What the law actually requires

DEQ cannot issue the permit unless the emission limit established for each
gen-set under that permit is equal to or lower than the emissions achieved by a
Tier 4-equivalent gen-set.

That is a performance standard, not an equipment mandate. The statute defines
Tier 4 equivalent by reference to three controls — selective catalytic reduction
or equivalent for NOx, a diesel oxidation catalyst or equivalent for carbon
monoxide, and a diesel particulate filter or equivalent for particulate matter.
The phrase "or equivalent" appears in each.

The statute itself does not use the term BACT. Separately, [DEQ Guidance Memo
APG-576](https://townhall.virginia.gov/L/ViewGDoc.cfm?gdid=5121), whose revised version took effect
9 April 2026, addresses presumptive best available control technology for
emergency and non-emergency diesel engine-generator sets at data center
stationary sources. The statute sets the permit limit; DEQ's guidance addresses
presumptive BACT for these generator sets.

## Why an enterprise data center owner should care

For applications covered by the statute, Tier 4-equivalent performance is now a
statutory minimum for control stringency. DEQ can still establish more stringent
emission limits through the applicable permitting process — BACT, LAER or other
requirements still apply.

One practical implication: emissions performance can now be screened during
equipment selection, because a permit covered by the statute cannot establish a
gen-set limit above the Tier 4-equivalent benchmark. That is our read of what
the requirement means in practice, not something the statute states.

## What we can and cannot conclude yet

We have verified what the statute says, what it defines, when it applies, and
the existence of both the size-based exemption and an additional
emissions-based exemption pathway.

We have not established what it costs. Aftertreatment systems plausibly affect
equipment price, physical footprint and procurement lead time, but we have found
no verified source quantifying any of those effects for Virginia data center
projects specifically. DCU will not publish estimates it cannot support.

The statute applies to an application submitted on or after 1 July 2026, but it
does not expressly address how that date applies to amendment or modification
requests involving permits issued earlier. DCU has not found published DEQ
guidance resolving that question.

Both are open questions we are working on.
