**Status:** In effect · public hearing on an extension, **12 October 2026** (6:30 p.m. session) · **Next:** Council vote on the extension, 26 October 2026 · **Checked:** 9 October 2026

> **If you are planning a data center in Charlotte.** A new application of any size cannot be
> accepted or approved while the pause lasts, and the city is moving to extend it beyond
> 5 November. The agenda does not say how long the extension would be, or whether adding to an
> existing facility counts as a new application.

## What is happening

On 8 June 2026 City Council adopted a 150-day moratorium on accepting, processing and approving
applications for new telecommunications and data storage facilities in the city and its
extraterritorial jurisdiction. It expires no later than 5 November 2026, or earlier if Council
approves new data center rules. The Council's planning committee recommended an extension on
8 September. State law requires a public hearing and a showing of new facts before a moratorium can
be extended.

The city gives two new facts: research it commissioned shows data center impacts are "variable and
complex", and Charlotte lacks specific regulatory tools such as capacity verification, monitoring
protocols and cooling standards.

## Who it affects

Charlotte's development ordinance treats all data centers the same "regardless of size, type, or
capacity". It defines a data center as a telecommunications and data storage facility, accessed only
by employees, housing computer systems, including cloud storage systems and server farms. Data centers
are currently permitted by right in several commercial, office, research, industrial and uptown
districts. Property and projects exempt under N.C.G.S. 160D-107(c) are not covered.

## If you have a project in Charlotte, check

1. Whether your project is a new application or a change to an approved one, and whether it is exempt
   under N.C.G.S. 160D-107(c).
2. How long the extension adopted on 26 October lasts.
3. What permanent standards the city proposes for capacity, monitoring and cooling.
