**Status:** In effect · extension approved unanimously 26 October 2026; runs 6 November 2026 to 18 October 2027 at the latest · **Next:** proposed permanent data center standards · **Checked:** 30 October 2026

> **If your organization runs, or plans, its own data center in Charlotte.** The extension exempts
> facilities that mainly support an organization's own operations, such as a company, hospital,
> university, government agency or telecommunications provider, provided they do not sell data services
> to outside users. Colocation and other facilities that serve outside customers remain paused. Confirm
> with Charlotte Planning that your project qualifies before you file.

## What changed

The pause adopted on 8 June 2026 was due to end by 5 November. After a public hearing on 12 October,
City Council approved an ordinance on 26 October extending it from 6 November 2026 to 18 October 2027,
or until Council adopts new data center rules, whichever comes first. The motion carried unanimously.

The extension stops the city accepting, processing or approving applications for new
telecommunications and data storage facilities. The ordinance says the conditions behind it are "the
emergence of data centers, in particular hyperscale facilities". It plans six to eight months of study
with a consultant, then three to five months to adopt any code or policy changes.

## Who is exempt

The ordinance states that the moratorium "shall not apply to facilities whose primary purpose is
supporting the internal operations of an institution, government entity, healthcare provider,
telecommunications provider, or other permitted principal uses so long as such data processing
activities are not offered as a primary service to off-site users." Projects exempt under N.C.G.S.
160D-107(c) are also excluded.

The ordinance does not define "primary purpose" or say how the city will judge whether a facility
qualifies, so ask before you rely on it. It also says nothing specific about expanding an existing
facility.

## If you have a project in Charlotte, check

1. Whether your facility's primary purpose is your own organization's operations, and whether any of its
   capacity is, or will be, offered to outside users.
2. How Charlotte Planning will confirm that a project qualifies for the exemption, and what to submit.
3. What permanent standards the city proposes for capacity, monitoring and cooling before October 2027.
