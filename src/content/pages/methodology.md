DataCenterUpdate provides source-transparent intelligence for people making enterprise data-center infrastructure decisions in the United States.

We focus on information that can affect cost, schedule, risk, and infrastructure choices — including equipment lead times, regulatory changes, power and utility developments, and related market conditions.

Wherever possible, we show not only the number or development, but where it came from, when it was published, and what the figure actually describes.

The goal is not to create another number. It is to provide enough context to understand whether that number is relevant to your situation.

## What we publish

For procurement intelligence, DCU publishes reported lead-time ranges and market observations tied to an identifiable equipment class, source, date, and measured population or scope.

For regulatory and infrastructure intelligence, we use statutes, permits, agency actions, utility proceedings, public filings, datasets, and other identifiable records, linking to the underlying source whenever practical.

DCU does not publish estimates of what your individual project will cost or present generalized equipment pricing as though it applies universally.

When a published source provides a forecast, estimate, or benchmark, we identify it as that source's figure rather than presenting it as a DCU forecast.

## Source types

Every published observation retains its source. We also classify sources so readers can understand the source's relationship to the information.

**R — Research**
Published research or recurring benchmark series describing broader market conditions, with the scope and methodology determined by the publisher.

**P — Practitioner**
Organizations involved directly in procuring, sourcing, specifying, or delivering equipment and therefore exposed to current supplier conditions and delivery schedules.

**V — Vendor**
Manufacturers, distributors, dealers, marketplaces, or other organizations that manufacture, sell, supply, or list the equipment or service being discussed.

**G — Government**
Government agencies, regulators, legislatures, and other official public bodies. These sources are authoritative for what an official law, rule, permit, filing, or agency action says.

These classifications describe **what kind of source something is, not how trustworthy it is.**

Different sources have different perspectives, methodologies, visibility, and commercial or institutional interests. The purpose of the classification is to make that context visible, not to rank one type of source above another.

## We do not blend unlike numbers

Two rules guide DCU's procurement reporting.

**We do not combine observations from different source types into one artificial market number.**

If two procurement organizations report materially different lead times, those observations remain visible separately rather than being collapsed into a single figure.

More importantly, **we do not average different equipment classes simply because they share a common name.**

A pad-mount distribution transformer, a substation transformer, and a generator step-up transformer are all transformers. They can differ substantially in capacity, voltage, construction, application, and manufacturing constraints.

A published figure labeled simply "transformer lead time" may accurately describe the population studied while still being difficult to apply to a specific enterprise project.

DCU preserves equipment-class distinctions wherever the source provides them. If a source does not define the equipment class, configuration, or population behind a figure, we say so.

## Verification

A figure that cannot be traced to a credible original source does not get published as a DCU observation.

Secondary reporting can help identify something worth investigating, but when the underlying document, dataset, filing, or original research is available, that is what DCU attempts to verify.

If the original source cannot be located, the context cannot be established, or the claim cannot be supported with sufficient confidence, we would rather leave a space blank than fill it with an uncertain number.

Primary public records are preferred for laws, regulations, permits, and government actions. For commercial research and industry benchmarks, DCU attempts to trace observations to the original publisher rather than repeat numbers through a chain of secondary articles.

Where practical, DCU also retains copies of or references to source material used in published analysis so figures can be checked later as sources change or disappear.

## Update cadence

Procurement observations are reviewed periodically, with major updates typically published quarterly.

Regulatory, utility, and infrastructure developments are reviewed as material changes occur.

Not every source updates on the same schedule, so the date attached to each observation matters as much as the number itself.

## Corrections

We want to hear about errors.

If DCU publishes a factual error, we correct it as soon as we can confirm the issue. Material corrections will note what changed so readers are not left wondering whether an article or data point was silently altered.
